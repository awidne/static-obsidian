# Pediatric Dermatology Lesions & Exanthems
## Background
Several dermatologic conditions are more common in pediatric populations. Some conditions overlap with [[Dermatology Lesions|dermatology lesions]] page.
## Conditions
##### Irritant Contact Dermatitis (Diaper Rash)
![[Pediatric Dermatology Lesions & Exanthems-1.png|400]]
###### Clinical Findings
- Erythema
- Shiny, red macules/patches
- No skin fold involvement
- Scaling possible
###### Pathophysiology
###### Diagnosis/Treatment
- Eliminate contact (eg. no-diaper time, frequent changes)
- Topical barriers
- Short term topical corticosteroids
##### Candidal Dermatitis
![[Pediatric Dermatology Lesions & Exanthems-2.png|400]]
###### Clinical Findings
- Erythematous macerated papules/plaques
- Satellite papules/pustules
- Skin fold involvement
- Rash lasts >3 days
###### Pathophysiology
- Secondary to C. albincans from fecal origin
###### Diagnosis/Treatment
- Antifungals
- No amoxicillin (will worsen)
##### Seborrheic Dermatitis (Cradle Cap)
![[Pediatric Dermatology Lesions & Exanthems-3.png|400]]
###### Clinical Findings
- Greasy, yellowish-white scale with erythema
- Predilection for nasolabial folds, eyebrows, scalp, external ear canals, chest
- Chronic relapsing course
- Worsens with stress, fatigue, neurological disease
- Dandruff is a mild scalp form
###### Pathophysiology
- Malassezia yeast metabolizes sebum triglycerides (in sebum rich areas such as face and scalp)
- Free fatty acids from metabolized TGs disrupt skin barrier
- Disrupted skin barrier allows for inflammatory activation
###### Diagnosis/Treatment
- Ketoconazole shampoo/cream
##### Neonatal / Infantile Acne
![[Pediatric Dermatology Lesions & Exanthems-4.png|400]]
###### Clinical Findings
- Papules/pustules on forehead, cheeks, and upper chest
- Starts age 2-3 weeks (neonatal) or after 3 months (infantile)
- Resolves spontaneously
###### Pathophysiology
###### Diagnosis/Treatment
- Resolves spontaneously, reassure
##### Milia
![[Pediatric Dermatology Lesions & Exanthems-5.png|400]]
###### Clinical Findings
- 1-2 mm firm, white, pearly pinpoint papules
###### Pathophysiology
- Keratin retained in follicles causes papules
###### Diagnosis/Treatment
- Self-resolves within a few weeks of life
##### Miliaria
![[Pediatric Dermatology Lesions & Exanthems-6.png|400]]
###### Clinical Findings
- Localized, pruritic vesicles
- Irritation, itching, rash types (depends on depth of trap)
###### Pathophysiology
- Plugged sweat gland ducts
###### Diagnosis/Treatment

###### Clinical Findings
- Slate grey patches on the lower trunk
- May look like bruises
- Presents between birth to 2-3 years of age
- More common in Asian/Black babies

###### Diagnosis/Treatment

![[Pediatric Dermatology Lesions & Exanthems-8.png|250]]![[Pediatric Dermatology Lesions & Exanthems-9.png|200]]
###### Clinical Findings
- Erythematous macules and papules

- Presents between 24 hours of life to 5-7 days
###### Pathophysiology
###### Diagnosis/Treatment
- Self resolving

###### Diagnosis/Treatment
