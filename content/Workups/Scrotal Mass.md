# Scrotal Mass
## Background
### Pathophysiology
## Differential Diagnosis
**Immediately emergent**
- [[Testicular Torsion|Testicular torsion]]

**Non-emergent**
- Epididymitis/epididymoorchitis
- Apendage torsion
- Incarcerated/strangulated inguinal hernia
- Traumatic hematoma
- Testicular cancer

**Benign**
- Spermatocele / epididymal cyst
- Hydrocele
- Varicocele (may be concerning if right sided)
## Initial Evaluation
### History
History should include:
- Onset and duration
- Associated symptoms including pain
- History of trauma, infection, surgery

History should rule out concerning features of testicular torsion. Suspicion of such should proceed [[Testicular Torsion|accordingly]].
### Physical Exam
Physical exam consist of palpation of the scrotum. A systematic approach should be used to assess:
- Scrotal fullness (size, "bag of worms") - suggestive of varicocele
- Unitlateral or bilateral diffuse swelling, uniform in consistency - hydrocele
- Localized fullness at head of epididymis - epididymal cyst of spermatocele
- FIrm, nontender nodule/mass on testis - testicular cancer
- Mass in paratesticular tissue or tail in epididymis - adenomatoid tumour

Transillumination of the scrotum may be helpful (hydrocele permits transillumination).
## Investigations
If the diagnosis is unclear from initial evaluation, a scrotal ultrasound should be performed. US is >95% sensitive for concerning intratesticular lesions, of which should be referred to urology.
